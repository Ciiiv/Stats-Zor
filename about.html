How it works

Every number on Stats-Zor is computed from public data by code you can read in the GitHub repo. Nothing is hand-entered except the cap figures listed under Value and worth. This page explains, section by section, what each part shows, how it's calculated, why it's built that way, and what it can't see.

Glossary

5v5
Five skaters a side, no power play. Most even-strength stats here are 5v5 only, because special teams change everything about shot rates.
xG (expected goals)
MoneyPuck's estimate of how likely each shot is to score, from its location, type, angle, rebound and rush status. Adding them up measures chance quality better than goals, which swing on luck and goaltending.
xGF / xGA
Expected goals for and against while the player is on the ice.
xGF%
xGF ÷ (xGF + xGA). 50% is break-even; above 50% means his team controls the chances while he's out there.
Per 60
A rate per 60 minutes of ice time, so players with different minutes can be compared fairly.
Relative (rel)
His team's results with him on the ice minus its results with him on the bench. It removes much of the "good team makes everyone look good" effect. A rel xGA/60 of −0.6 means the team allows 0.6 fewer expected goals per 60 with him on.
CF% / CA
Corsi: all shot attempts (on goal, missed and blocked). A rough possession measure.
HD xGA
Expected goals against from high-danger shots, mostly from the slot and crease.
Zone starts
Where a player's shifts begin on a faceoff. A high own-zone share means harder, defensive deployment.
ixG, ig
Individual expected goals and goals: the player's own shots only.
Goals above expected
Goals minus individual xG. Positive means he finishes better than an average shooter from the same spots.
Percentile
The share of the comparison pool he's better than. 90th means better than 90% of them.
Wins above replacement
Stats-Zor's estimate of how many wins a player adds over a replacement-level call-up. See Value and worth.
Era-adjusted
Scoring rescaled to a common league-wide scoring level, so a 1980s point and a 2020s point can be compared. See Career vs. career.
NHL EDGE
The NHL's puck and player tracking (2021-22 on): skating speed, speed bursts, distance skated, shot speed and zone time.
GSAx
Goals saved above expected, for goalies: the expected goals of every shot he faced minus the goals he allowed. +10 means he stopped 10 more than an average goalie would have on the same shots.
Sv% vs expected
His save % minus the save % an average goalie would post on his shots (1 − xGA ÷ shots on goal), in percentage points.
GSAA
Goals saved above average: shots faced × (his save % − league save %). Works back to 1955-56, before expected goals existed.
Quality start
A start with a save % at or above the league average, or .885+ when he faced 20 shots or fewer. A really bad start is under .850.
Age
Age on February 1 of the season, the same convention Hockey-Reference uses.

Pools and percentiles

Most colors, scores and ranks are percentiles against a pool of players at the same position in the same season. Forwards are compared with forwards and defensemen with defensemen, never across positions.

  • Regulars (default): at least 12 minutes a game for forwards or 16 for defensemen, and at least 35% of the season's games (minimum 5).
  • Top-pair D / top-six F: at least 20 minutes for defensemen or 16 for forwards. A tougher comparison group.
percentile = (players below him + half of players tied with him) ÷ pool size × 100

For stats where lower is better (xGA/60, giveaways, PK xGA/60 and so on) the percentile is flipped, so a higher percentile always means better. Faceoff percentile is computed among centers with 100+ draws only.

Limits: percentiles say how a player ranks, not by how much. Near the middle of the pack, a small difference in the raw stat can move a percentile a lot.

Profile

The two cards at the top show the season line (goals, assists, points, games), ice time, 5v5 xGF% and relative xGF%, plus three scores from the two-way composite: defense, offense and role difficulty. Trophies and Cups come from the NHL's records. Age, size and handedness come from NHL bios.

Tools (NHL EDGE)

For 2021-22 on, each card shows tracking data from the NHL's EDGE system: top skating speed, number of 20+ mph bursts, hardest shot, distance skated per game, and share of 5v5 time spent in the offensive zone. Percentiles are the NHL's own, forwards against forwards and defensemen against defensemen.

Trend: his top speed and 20+ mph bursts per game this season against his best earlier EDGE season (20+ games). It's flagged Slowing if top speed is down 0.5 mph or more, or bursts per game are down 25% or more. Speed usually fades before production does, so it's an early sign for older players.

Why it's not in the value model: speed and shot power describe how a player plays, not how much he helps. Fast players can drive play poorly and slow ones can be excellent, and what tools produce (chances, goals, suppression) is already counted. EDGE is used for style instead: in the stat board's "most similar" match and in the trend.

Limits: top speed and hardest shot are single peak moments from one game. Bursts and distance are season totals, so they depend on games and minutes played. No EDGE data exists before 2021-22.

Part one · how he played

Two-way composite

Three scores, each an average of percentiles, then combined with the weight sliders.

Defensemen Defense = 75% × avg(xGA/60, rel xGA/60, HD xGA/60, CA/60, PK xGA/60) + 25% × avg(blocks/60, takeaways/60, DZ giveaways/60 [lower better], hits/60) Offense = avg(xGF/60, rel xGF/60, ixG/60, 5v5 points/60, PP points/60) Role = avg(TOI, PK TOI, own-zone start %, team xGF% without him [lower = harder]) Forwards Defense = 80% × avg(xGA/60, rel xGA/60, HD xGA/60, PK xGA/60) + 20% × avg(takeaways/60, giveaways/60 [lower better]) Offense = avg(goals/60, 5v5 points/60, ixG/60, xGF/60, rel xGF/60, PP points/60) Role = avg(TOI, PK TOI, own-zone start %, team xGF% without him, faceoff % [centers only]) Composite = (Defense × wD + Offense × wO + Role × wR) ÷ (wD + wO + wR)

Why: raw points reward offense only. This puts suppression, deployment and teammate context on equal footing, and the sliders let you decide how much each matters. "Role difficulty" credits players who carry hard minutes on weak teams. A missing piece (no PK time, say) is skipped, not counted as zero.

Limits: hits, blocks, takeaways and giveaways are recorded by arena scorers and vary by building, so they get a small weight. On-ice stats are shared with linemates.

Stat board

A grid of raw values, each colored by its percentile in the pool: blue is good, orange is poor. On role columns, blue means a harder job. Two ways to fill it:

  • Top-ranked: the highest composite scores at his position.
  • Most similar: the players whose percentile profile across every board column is closest to his.
match % = 100 − √(average of (his percentile − their percentile)² across columns) (players missing more than 40% of the columns are skipped) when both players have NHL EDGE data, top speed, 20+ mph bursts and shot speed percentiles are added as style columns at half weight each

Your comparison player and anyone you add always stay on the board.

Head to head

A radar of eight percentiles, each player against his own position group. Defensemen: ice time, own-zone starts, xGA/60, rel xGA/60, PK xGA/60, blocks + takeaways (average of the two percentiles), rel xGF/60 and 5v5 points/60. Forwards: ice time, goals/60, points/60, ixG/60, rel xGF/60, rel xGA/60, PK xGA/60, and faceoffs for centers or takeaways for wingers. Lower-is-better stats are flipped, so farther out is always better. The table beside it shows raw values, with a dot on the better one.

Shot environment and deployment

  • Left chart: 5v5 xGF/60 against xGA/60 for every regular at the position. Gray lines mark the medians. Up and left means more chances for and fewer against.
  • Right chart: own-zone start share against relative xGF%. Right means tougher starts; up means the team is better with him on the ice.

Shot maps

Unblocked shot attempts (on goal, missed and goals; MoneyPuck's shot file doesn't include blocked shots) in the offensive zone, in 5 × 5 ft cells. Regular season only; empty-net shots are left out.

  • Where he shoots: his share of attempts from each cell minus the league share for his position. It's full blue at 1.2 percentage points more than average and full orange at 1.2 less. Rings mark goals.
  • Finishing: goals minus expected goals per cell, for cells with at least 3 attempts. Full color at ±2.5 goals.
  • Slot share: attempts from 20 ft in front of the net out to the goal line, within 10 ft of center.

Situations: all, 5v5 (5 skaters a side), or power play (his team has more skaters).

Limits: cells with few shots are noisy, especially in finishing view. Coordinates are MoneyPuck's arena-adjusted locations.

Linemates and partners

Built from MoneyPuck's 5v5 line and pair data: forward trios for forwards, defense pairs for defensemen.

together = sum of every line/pair containing both players him without partner = his 5v5 totals − together partner without him = partner's 5v5 totals − together

Teammates with at least 30 minutes together are shown. xGF% is left blank under 10 minutes.

Limits: "without" is everything else he played, mostly with other partners, so it isn't a clean test. Forward-and-defense combinations aren't available in the data.

Team swap

Projects a player's 5v5 impact onto another team in place of one of its players.

his impact = his on-ice rate − his team's rate with him on the bench (xGF/60 and xGA/60) him on new team = that team's rates without the replaced player + his impact xG differential = (xGF/60 − xGA/60) × his 5v5 minutes per game × 82 ÷ 60 wins ≈ xG differential ÷ 6

Limits: it assumes a player's impact moves with him unchanged. New partners, coaches, systems and roles all change that in reality. Treat it as the direction and rough size of the change.

Part two · how he's used

Role and fit

Slots, every game: from MoneyPuck's game-by-game data, each team's players are ranked by 5v5 ice time that night.

  • Even strength: forwards 1–3 = 1st line, 4–6 = 2nd, 7–9 = 3rd, 10+ = 4th. Defensemen 1–2 = 1st pair, 3–4 = 2nd, 5+ = 3rd.
  • Power play: the top 5 skaters by 5-on-4 time are PP1, the next 5 are PP2 (30+ seconds needed).
  • Penalty kill: within forwards and defensemen separately, the top 2 by 4-on-5 time are PK1 and the next 2 are PK2.
  • Zone nights: 2+ more offensive- than defensive-zone starts makes an offensive-zone-heavy night, the reverse a defensive-zone-heavy night, otherwise balanced.

Season role labels: his average slot across games. PP1 means PP1 in at least half his games; PP2 means a PP unit in at least half. Spot PP means at least 15% of games. Same for PK.

Role averages: the league's combined 5v5 results for everyone in each slot. Every "Role avg" in the tables comes from these.

Three ways to judge fit:

5v5 results = weighted percentile of forwards: xGF% 35%, rel xGF% 25%, 5v5 points/60 40% defensemen: xGF% 35%, rel xGF% 25%, rel xGA/60 25%, 5v5 points/60 15% Overall value = percentile of wins above replacement per 82 games (Part 3 model) Both are blended over up to 3 seasons: weights 3, 2, 1 (most recent first) × games played. fits slot = slots + 0.5 − slots × percentile ÷ 100 (4 slots for forwards, 3 for D) → top quarter of forwards ≈ 1st line ... bottom quarter ≈ 4th line Against his role = his 5v5 xGF% − league xGF% for the slots he played, weighted by his minutes in each

Flags:

  • Even strength and overall: his average slot vs. his fit slot. A gap of 0.6 slot or more either way is flagged.
  • Against his role: ±1.5 points of xGF% or more is flagged.
  • Power play: needs 0.75+ PP minutes a game. PP points/60 in the 70th percentile or higher is PP1 production, 40th–70th is PP2. Unused players in the 70th percentile or higher for 5v5 scoring are "worth a PP look."
  • Penalty kill: needs 0.75+ PK minutes a game. PK xGA/60 in the 65th percentile or higher is PK1 results, 40th–65th is PK2. Unused players in the 70th percentile or higher for rel xGA/60 are "PK candidates."
  • Zone starts: "Can take own-zone starts" needs 50+ minutes on defensive-zone-heavy nights, a defense score of 60+, and xGF% no more than 4 points worse than on offensive-zone nights. "Best sheltered" means an offense score of 60+ with a defense score under 45.

Why three views: a power-play specialist or elite finisher can read low at 5v5 but high overall. A first-liner facing top competition can read low on both but hold up against his role. Reading all three together avoids one-number verdicts.

Limits:

  • Competition: who a player was on the ice against isn't in the public data, which biases 5v5 numbers against first-liners. "Against his role" is the best available correction.
  • Cause and effect: coaches move players partly because of form, injuries and matchups, and promotion usually brings better linemates.
  • Small samples: under 30 minutes is left blank, and one season is noisy, hence the three-season blend.

Team deployment card

Every regular on a team with at least 25% of the season's games. It shows his average slot ("used as"), 5v5 fit, overall fit and vs. role, with power-play and penalty-kill use and fit, and zone fit, all as defined above. The even-strength flag here uses overall fit. Traded players show their whole season.

Part three · what he's worth

Value and worth

Stats-Zor's own wins-above-replacement model. It's simpler than commercial models like Evolving-Hockey's GAR, but every piece is visible.

Goals above replacement (per season) 5v5 offense = 0.5 × (rel xGF/60 − replacement) × 5v5 hours 5v5 defense = 0.5 × (replacement − rel xGA/60) × 5v5 hours Finishing = 0.5 × (goals − individual xG), all situations Power play = 0.3 × (on-ice PP xGF/60 − replacement) × PP hours Penalty kill = 0.25 × (replacement − on-ice PK xGA/60) × PK hours Penalties = 0.15 × (penalties drawn − penalties taken) Replacement = 20th percentile among regulars at the position (80th for the against stats), roughly what a call-up provides. Wins = goals ÷ 6

Why these weights: on-ice stats are shared by five skaters, so 5v5 and special-teams credit is scaled down. Only half of finishing is counted, because shooting luck regresses. A penalty drawn is worth about 0.15 goals, roughly the value of a power play.

Price of a win = 32 teams × (cap × 0.88 skater share − 20 skaters × minimum salary) ÷ all positive wins produced that season Worth = minimum salary + max(0, wins per 82 games) × price of a win % of cap = worth ÷ that season's cap

Caps used ($M): 2008-09 56.7 · 09-10 56.8 · 10-11 59.4 · 11-12 64.3 · 12-13 60.0 (lockout) · 13-14 64.3 · 14-15 69.0 · 15-16 71.4 · 16-17 73.0 · 17-18 75.0 · 18-19 79.5 · 19-20 to 21-22 81.5 · 22-23 82.5 · 23-24 83.5 · 24-25 88.0 · 25-26 95.5 · 26-27 104.0 · 27-28 113.5 (projected), then an assumed +5% a year. Minimum salary follows each season's CBA figure, rising to $1M by 2029-30.

Limits: stars come out "worth" far more than their pay. That's normal for value models, because the max salary, entry-level and RFA contracts keep the best players underpaid, so read the dollars as relative value. A partial current season uses each player's pace over 82 games, so early-season figures swing. Goalies have their own version, under Goalie value.

Fair-value contract

Starting point = his wins per 82, blended over 3 seasons (weights 3, 2, 1 × games) Each future year: wins × age factor for that age ≤23 ×1.10 · 24–26 ×1.03 · 27–28 ×1.00 · 29–30 ×0.95 · 31–32 ×0.90 · 33+ ×0.85 Worth that year = minimum salary + wins × price of a win (scaled to that year's cap) Fair AAV for an N-year deal = average worth over those N years

Terms run 1–7 years, from next season. The current CBA allows 7 years to re-sign with your own team and 6 as a free agent.

Limits: this is what his projected value would justify, not a prediction of what he'll sign for. RFA rights, arbitration, the max-salary cap, cap crunches and market quirks all move real contracts.

Trade machine

  • Players: his blended wins per 82 × the share of this season left, plus the projected wins for each future season you count ("years left"). Worth is priced the same way. Goalies are on the rosters too, valued with the goalie model in wins per season at their own workload, and priced at the goalie price of a win.
  • Cap hits: if you enter one, cost = cap hit × (share of this season left + future years), and surplus = worth − cost. Once every player has a cap hit, the verdict switches from value to surplus.
  • Draft picks: expected wins over a pick's cost-controlled years, by overall pick number. "Early" picks are #4, "middle" #16 and "late" #28 in each round. The value drops 10% for each year beyond the next draft.
  • Prospects: drafted in the last five drafts, under 100 NHL games, and not on an NHL roster. They get the value of their draft slot, reduced 15% for each full year since the draft. They're listed under their drafting team (Arizona picks under Utah).
  • Verdict: "About even" if the gap is under 0.4 wins ($1.5M in surplus mode) or under 8% of everything traded.
pick value (wins) = 6 × e^(−0.045 × (pick number − 1)) + 0.15 → #1 ≈ 6.2 · #16 ≈ 3.2 · #32 ≈ 1.6 · #64 ≈ 0.5

Limits: who actually owns which picks isn't in the free data, so picks are generic. Cap space, retained salary, trade clauses and team needs aren't checked. Contracts aren't included; cap hits you type stay in your browser.

Part four · vs. history

Career vs. career

League scoring rate = all goals ÷ (team games ÷ 2), from NHL team totals each season Era-adjusted points = points × 6.0 ÷ that season's league scoring rate Per 82 = adjusted points ÷ games × 82

6.0 goals a game is a neutral baseline. Since the 1940s, league scoring has ranged from about 4.8 goals a game (early 1950s) to 8.0 (early 1980s). "Same age" freezes both players at the main player's latest completed age. "Same career stage" uses his number of full seasons (40%+ of the schedule played). Ice time only exists from 1997-98 on, and expected-goal stats from 2008-09 on.

Limits: era adjustment doesn't account for roster size, schedule differences or how assists were awarded in different eras. Trophies are winners only; Norris and Hart voting finishes aren't in the NHL's public data.

Career arcs and comps

The arcs chart plots every season by age, for any measure in the buttons (era-adjusted points or goals per 82, ice time, games, or relative xGF%/xGA/60 for 2008+).

Comps: players at the same position with enough games (100, or half of his total, whichever is more) by his current age, compared through that age.

features (weight): era-adj points/82 (1), era-adj goals/82 (0.6), ice time (1, if both have it for 60%+ of games), rel xGF% and rel xGA/60 (1 each, if both have 100+ games of 2008+ data) each feature standardized by its spread across candidates distance = √(weighted average of squared differences); closest = smallest distance

Limits: it compares production and on-ice impact, not size, style or role. Older comps are matched on scoring only.

Goalies · goalies.html

Goalie page basics

Goalies have their own page with the same four parts, because almost nothing a skater is judged on applies to them. Searching a goalie's name on the skater page sends you there, and the other way around. Goalies are only compared with goalies.

Pools: Regulars have at least 25% of the season's games (minimum 5); Starters have started at least 45% of them. Percentiles work exactly as for skaters, flipped where lower is better (GAA, rebounds).

Save % = 1 − goals against ÷ shots on goal Expected save % = 1 − expected goals against ÷ shots on goal Sv% vs expected (pts) = 100 × (expected goals against − goals against) ÷ shots on goal GSAx = expected goals against − goals against GSAx/60 = GSAx ÷ minutes × 60 Danger sv% vs exp. = 100 × (xGA − GA) ÷ shots, within MoneyPuck's low, mid or high danger group Rebounds vs exp. /60 = (rebounds allowed − expected rebounds) ÷ hours (lower is better) Freezes vs exp. /60 = (pucks frozen − expected freezes) ÷ hours

Expected goals against include missed shots, which can't score, so they're compared with shots on goal for the expected save %. Empty-net time doesn't count: the goalie is on the bench.

Limits: MoneyPuck's expected-goals model knows shot location, type, angle, rebounds and rushes, but not screens, deflections in front or passes right before the shot. Goalies behind teams that allow a lot of those are shortchanged a little. And over one season, save % is roughly half skill and half luck, so single-season goalie numbers swing far more than skater numbers.

Goalie composite and stat board

Stopping = avg(GSAx/60, Sv% vs expected, 5v5 GSAx/60) Danger & situations = avg(high-, mid- and low-danger sv% vs expected, PK GSAx/60, rebounds vs expected) Workload = avg(starts, shots faced /60, xGA/60 faced) Composite = weighted average of the three, using the sliders (default 50 / 30 / 20)

Workload is the goalie version of role difficulty: carrying the crease and facing a heavy, dangerous load is harder than a light backup schedule. Presets: Balanced, Pure saves (no workload) and Workhorse.

Stat board: the same color-coded percentile matrix as for skaters, with workload, results, danger, situation, puck-control and record columns. "Most similar" uses the root-mean-square percentile difference across the save and workload columns (not wins or shutouts, which depend on the team).

Head to head: a percentile radar (overall, 5v5, high/mid/low danger, PK, rebound control and workload) and a side-by-side table. Expected save % and xGA/60 describe the shots he faced, so they aren't marked as wins.

Shot environment

Left: xGA/60 faced against GSAx/60, so you can see whether a goalie's numbers came behind a strong or a leaky defense. Right: shots on goal per 60 against expected goals per unblocked shot, which separates volume from danger. Lines mark the medians (and zero GSAx).

Shot maps

Built from the same MoneyPuck shot files as skater maps, but grouped by the goalie who faced each shot: 5 × 5 ft cells across his defensive zone, for all situations, 5v5 and the penalty kill. Where shots came from compares his share of shots from each cell with the league total for all goalies (orange = more often than average). Saves vs expected shows expected goals minus goals allowed in each cell with 5+ shots (blue = better than average from there).

Goalie swap

His rate = (xGA − GA) ÷ xGA this season (goals saved per expected goal) Goals against there = the replaced goalie's xGA × (1 − his rate) Goals saved = replaced goalie's actual goals against − goals against there Wins ≈ goals saved ÷ 6

Using the rate per expected goal (rather than per 60) makes his saves scale with how many chances the new team actually allows. The reverse swap is shown too.

Limits: assumes save talent travels unchanged and the team in front of him plays the same. Goalie systems, rebound handling and puck-playing interact with the defense in ways this doesn't capture.

Workload and fit

Starts and decisions come from the NHL's game-by-game goalie logs; shots, goals and expected goals from MoneyPuck's game-by-game goalie file.

Start share = his starts ÷ his team's games (while he was on each team, if traded) Role 65%+ workhorse starter · 50%+ starter · 40–50% 1A · 28–40% 1B · 12–28% backup · under 12% spot duty Results fit = GSAx/60 blended over 3 seasons (weights 3, 2, 1 × minutes), ranked among this season's regular goalies: top third = starter results · middle = tandem · bottom = backup Flag results level above his role → "Results earn more starts" below → "Starting more than his results support"

Rest: each start is grouped by days since his previous start: back-to-back (started yesterday too), 1, 2 or 3+ days of rest, with save %, expected save % and GSAx/60, next to the league rate for the same rest. Back-to-backs also count the team's second nights and how many he started. Quality starts use the Hockey Prospectus definition in the glossary. Pulled counts starts where another goalie came in and he played under 55 minutes. Home and road starts are split too.

Form through the season

GSAx per start averaged over his last 10 starts (shown from his 5th start on), by date. Faint dots are single starts.

Team goalie card

Every goalie who appeared for the team: games, starts, share and role, save %, GSAx, GSAx/60, quality-start rate, results fit and flag.

Limits: who starts depends on contracts, injuries, schedule and trust as much as on results, and a backup's numbers often come from easier nights. Rest splits are small samples.

Goalie value and fair contract

Goals above replacement, for each situation (5v5, penalty kill, everything else) = 0.5 × (GSAx − replacement rate × hours in that situation) Replacement rate = 20th percentile GSAx/60 among regular goalies, in that situation Wins = goals ÷ 6 Price of a goalie win = 32 × (cap × 0.12 goalie share − 2 goalies × minimum salary) ÷ all positive goalie wins that season Worth = minimum salary + max(0, his wins this season) × price of a goalie win (an unfinished season is projected at his pace)

Why half credit: GSAx is the goalie's own number, but a season of it is roughly half signal and half noise and team effects the model can't see, so only half is credited. That also keeps goalie wins on a scale comparable with skater wins: in a typical season goalies produce about 13–15% of all positive wins, close to their 12% share of the cap. (Dollar worth barely depends on this weight: doubling everyone's wins would halve the price of a win.)

Why not per 82 games: skater worth uses wins per 82, because skaters play most games. No goalie plays 82, so goalie worth uses the wins he actually produced, and projections use his own workload.

Rate = wins per 82 games, 3-season blend (weights 3, 2, 1 × games) Kept share = shots faced in those seasons ÷ (shots faced + 3,000) Projected = league-average goalie rate + kept share × (his rate − league-average rate) Workload = his games per season, same 3/2/1 blend (up to 65; an unfinished season counts in proportion to how much of it is played) Wins a year = projected rate × workload ÷ 82, then the age factor for each year Age factors ≤24 ×1.08 · 25–27 ×1.03 · 28–31 ×1.00 · 32–33 ×0.95 · 34–35 ×0.90 · 36+ ×0.85

Trusting repeated results: one season as a starter (about 1,600 shots) keeps roughly a third of his edge over an average goalie; three full seasons (about 4,800) keep roughly 60%. So a single hot season doesn't project like an established elite goalie, while three strong seasons in a row still do. A call-up with a few hundred shots projects close to average. The 3,000-shot setting (GOALIE_SHOTS_K) is a judgment call, roughly where save % is as much skill as luck. Skaters keep their own, simpler projection.

Goalies peak later and hold it longer than skaters, so the age curve is shifted. Fair AAV for a term is the average projected worth over those years, as for skaters. The trade machine uses the same projection, in wins per season.

Limits: the noise in goalie seasons means projections are much less certain than for skaters; the pull toward average only partly handles it, and the workload assumes his recent share of starts continues. Contracts aren't known; type a cap hit to compare.

Goalie careers, arcs and comps

League save % (each season) = 1 − goals against ÷ shots, all goalies with shot counts Save % vs league (×1000) = 1000 × (his save % − league save %) GSAA = shots × (1 − league save %) − goals against Era-adjusted GAA = GAA × 3.0 ÷ that season's league GAA

The NHL has recorded shots against since 1955-56, so save % and GSAA start there; earlier seasons have record, shutouts and GAA only. Expected goals (GSAx) start in 2008-09. "Same age" and "same career stage" work as for skaters, with a full season being 20+ games.

Comps: goalies with at least 40 games (or half of his total) by his age, compared on save % vs league (weight 1.2, if both have shot counts for most games), era-adjusted GAA (0.8), games played (0.6) and GSAx/60 (1, if both have 50+ hours of 2008+ data), each standardized by its spread.

Limits: wins, shutouts and GAA depend heavily on the team. League save % rose steadily for decades with equipment and coaching, so save % vs league is the fairest cross-era measure here, but it still doesn't know shot quality before 2008-09.

A GitHub Actions job rebuilds the data every morning (about 7 a.m. Eastern) after MoneyPuck's overnight update. Past seasons are kept; the current season is refreshed each day.
