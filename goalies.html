      The season

      The core number is goals saved above expected (GSAx): the goals an average goalie would have allowed on the shots he faced, given where and how they were taken, minus the goals he actually allowed. Above zero means he stopped more than expected.

        Goalie swap

        How this works

        Put him behind another team's defense in place of one of its goalies. His rate of goals saved per expected goal is applied to the chances that team actually allowed in front of the goalie he replaces, and the other way around.

        Team goalie card

        How this works

        Every goalie who played for a team: how the starts were split, and where each one's results say he fits. "Fit" ranks goals saved above expected per 60, blended over three seasons, among regular goalies: top third is starter quality, middle third tandem, bottom third backup.

        Part three · value · Stats-Zor model

        What he's worth

        Goals saved above a replacement-level goalie over the same minutes, credited at half (the other half is noise and team), turned into wins and priced with the goalie share of the salary cap. It estimates fair value; it doesn't know actual contracts.

        Part four · careers across eras · NHL data

        Against history

        Raw save percentage and goals-against average from different eras aren't comparable: league save % sank to about .875 in the mid-1980s, peaked near .915 in the mid-2010s and has slipped to about .895 since. Era figures compare each season with that season's league average. The NHL has counted shots since 1955-56, so save % starts there; expected goals start in 2008-09.
